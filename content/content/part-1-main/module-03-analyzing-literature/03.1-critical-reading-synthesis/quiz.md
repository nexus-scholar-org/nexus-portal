### Question 1 — Conceptual (Remember)
**[Multiple Choice]**

What is a Synthesis Matrix (Concept Matrix)?

- A) A spreadsheet that lists papers alphabetically by author name.
- B) A table where rows represent themes/concepts and columns represent individual papers, showing what each paper contributes to each theme.
- C) A grading rubric for assessing the quality of individual papers.
- D) A mathematical formula for calculating a paper's impact factor.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — The Synthesis Matrix organizes the literature by themes, not by papers. This forces thematic thinking and reveals gaps.

</details>

### Question 2 — Conceptual (Understand)
**[Multiple Choice]**

What is the key difference between a "summary" literature review and a "synthesis" literature review?

- A) A summary is shorter; a synthesis is longer.
- B) A summary describes each paper individually; a synthesis identifies patterns, agreements, disagreements, and gaps across the body of literature.
- C) A summary cites papers; a synthesis does not.
- D) A summary is for the introduction; a synthesis is for the conclusion.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — A summary reads like a list of book reports. A synthesis reads like an analysis of a conversation — it tells you what the collective evidence says, not what each speaker said individually.

</details>

### Question 3 — Application (Apply)
**[Multiple Choice]**

You are building a Synthesis Matrix and notice that for the theme "Algorithmic Fairness," only 1 out of 12 papers has an entry. All other cells in that row are empty. What does this tell you?

- A) That algorithmic fairness is not important to the field.
- B) That you are building the matrix incorrectly.
- C) That there is a significant gap in the literature — most studies have not addressed this issue, and it could be a valuable angle for your thesis.
- D) That you should remove the theme from your matrix.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C** — Empty cells are data. A nearly empty row reveals a systematic gap in the literature, which is exactly the kind of insight a good literature review should surface.

</details>

### Question 4 — Synthesis Moves (Analyze)
**[Short Answer]**

Read the following paragraph and identify which synthesis move (Convergence, Divergence, Gap, or Evolution) each sentence represents:

*"Several studies have found that deep learning outperforms traditional machine learning for image classification (Smith 2020; Jones 2021; Lee 2022). However, Patel (2023) demonstrated that this advantage disappears when models are tested on multi-site data. Notably, no study has examined the impact of training data diversity on model fairness."*

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

1. "Several studies have found that deep learning outperforms..." → **Convergence** (multiple studies agree)
2. "However, Patel (2023) demonstrated that this advantage disappears..." → **Divergence** (one study challenges the majority)
3. "Notably, no study has examined..." → **Gap** (an area no one has studied)

</details>

### Question 5 — Extraction Template (Evaluate)
**[Multiple Choice]**

Why is it critical to use a standardized Extraction Template when reading papers for a literature review?

- A) Because your advisor requires it for the thesis format.
- B) Because without consistent data points across all papers, you cannot build a meaningful synthesis matrix — you would be comparing incomparable notes.
- C) Because it makes your notes look more professional.
- D) Because it prevents you from reading too many papers.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — The template enforces comparability. If you extract the same data points (research question, methodology, sample, key findings, limitations) from every paper, patterns become visible. Without this structure, synthesis is impossible.

</details>
