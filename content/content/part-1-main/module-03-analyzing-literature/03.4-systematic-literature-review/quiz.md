### Question 1 — Conceptual (Remember)
**[Multiple Choice]**

What does PRISMA stand for?

- A) Protocol for Research in Systematic Meta-Analysis
- B) Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses
- C) Peer-Reviewed Index of Scientific Methods and Applications
- D) Practical Review of Integrated Scholarly Meta-Analysis

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — PRISMA provides a standardized checklist and flow diagram for reporting systematic reviews.

</details>

### Question 2 — Conceptual (Understand)
**[Multiple Choice]**

What is the primary difference between a narrative literature review and a systematic literature review?

- A) A narrative review is longer.
- B) A systematic review uses a pre-defined, reproducible protocol with documented inclusion/exclusion criteria, while a narrative review relies on the author's subjective judgment.
- C) A narrative review only covers old papers.
- D) A systematic review doesn't require citations.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — The defining feature of a systematic review is reproducibility through a pre-defined protocol.

</details>

### Question 4 — Methodology (Analyze)
**[Short Answer]**

A researcher conducts a systematic review. After running their search, they find 500 papers. They read through the results and decide to add 10 more papers they found on a colleague's reading list that were not in the original search results. Is this methodologically acceptable in a systematic review? Why or why not?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

This is a gray area but must be transparently documented. PRISMA 2020 allows for "records identified from other sources" (e.g., citation snowballing, expert recommendations), but these must be reported separately in the flow diagram under "Other Methods." The researcher cannot simply add them to the database search results without documentation, as this undermines reproducibility.

</details>

---

### Question 5 — Critical Thinking (Evaluate)
**[Multiple Choice]**

Why is it methodologically important to define inclusion and exclusion criteria *before* conducting the search?

- A) Because it saves time during screening.
- B) To prevent confirmation bias — defining criteria after seeing results allows the researcher to unconsciously include papers supporting their hypothesis and exclude contradictory ones.
- C) Because PRISMA requires exactly 5 criteria.
- D) Because search engines require boolean criteria to function.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — Pre-registration of criteria is the cornerstone of systematic review methodology. It prevents post-hoc rationalization and ensures the review is driven by the protocol, not by the researcher's biases.

</details>
