# Module 1.2: Reference Management & BibTeX
**Series:** Modern Research Methodologies
**Module:** Reading & Managing Knowledge

---

## Scene 1: The Hook (≤ 30 seconds)

**[Visual]**
- A student hunched over a laptop at 2 AM, furiously flipping between a paper and an APA formatting guide, checking where the commas go.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"You have just finished writing a 30-page manuscript. It took six months. Now, you have to write the bibliography. 

You spend six hours making sure every comma, period, and italic matches the formatting guide perfectly. You submit the paper. Three months later, the journal rejects it. You decide to submit to a different journal—but they use IEEE format instead of APA. Now you have to reformat all 100 citations by hand.

If you ever type a citation manually, you are doing it wrong. Let the machines do it."

---

## Scene 2: What is a Reference Manager? (2–3 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- A diagram showing the "Database" (Reference Manager) in the center. Metadata flows in from the web, and formatted citations flow out to a Word/LaTeX document.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"A reference manager is a database for your research. Instead of typing '(Smith, 2020)' in your document, you insert a placeholder. The reference manager stores all the metadata—authors, title, year, journal—and when you click 'compile,' it automatically replaces the placeholder with the perfectly formatted citation.

If you change journals, you just select a new style from a drop-down menu. The manager reformats your entire 100-item bibliography in half a second."

---

## Scene 3: The Big Three (2–4 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- Logos of Zotero, Mendeley, and EndNote.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Which one should you use? 

**EndNote** is the legacy choice. It's expensive and a bit clunky, though your university might pay for it. 
**Mendeley** is owned by Elsevier. It has a great PDF reader, but it tries to lock you into their ecosystem.
**Zotero** is open-source, free, and incredibly fast. It has the best browser extension for saving papers. Unless your lab forces you to use something else, we highly recommend Zotero."

---

## Scene 4: Under the Hood — BibTeX (3–5 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- A block of code appears showing a standard BibTeX entry for an article.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"But how does the software actually store this data? For much of academia, the underlying format is called BibTeX. It is a plain-text format used natively by LaTeX. 

Look at this structure. It starts with the entry type—`@article`—followed by the **Citation Key**. The citation key is the most important part. This is your unique ID for the paper. Usually, it's the author's last name and the year, like `Smith2020`. 

Then, you have the fields: author, title, journal, year. 

Because it is plain text, BibTeX is universally compatible. If Zotero disappears tomorrow, your `.bib` file will still work perfectly in a thousand other programs."

---

## Scene 5: The Ingestion Workflow (2 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- Screen recording of a user on a journal website. They click a button in their browser, and the metadata and PDF are instantly saved.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Never type metadata by hand. Never. 

Install the browser extension for your reference manager. When you find a paper you like, click the button. The software will extract the metadata, download the PDF, attach it to the entry, and generate the BibTeX citation key automatically."

---

## Scene 6: Summary (≤ 30 seconds)

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Let's recap:
1. **Stop typing citations:** Use a reference manager to separate the data from the formatting.
2. **Choose wisely:** Zotero is the open-source champion.
3. **BibTeX:** The universal, plain-text standard for bibliographic data.
4. **Automate:** Use browser extensions to capture metadata instantly."
