### Question 1 — Conceptual (Remember)
**[Multiple Choice]**

What does the acronym FINER stand for in the context of evaluating a research question?

- A) Fast, Intelligent, New, Efficient, Reproducible
- B) Feasible, Interesting, Novel, Ethical, Relevant
- C) Focused, Integrated, Narrow, Evidence-based, Rigorous
- D) Formulated, Indexed, Needed, Evaluated, Referenced

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — Feasible, Interesting, Novel, Ethical, Relevant. These five criteria must all be met for a research question to be worth pursuing.

</details>

### Question 2 — Conceptual (Understand)
**[Multiple Choice]**

A student says their research question is: "Machine learning in healthcare." According to this lesson, what is wrong with this statement?

- A) It uses too many technical terms.
- B) It is a broad topic, not a research question. It lacks specificity about population, method, and outcome.
- C) It is too narrow and should be expanded.
- D) Nothing is wrong; this is a valid research question.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — This is a topic (a broad area of interest), not a research question. A proper question must specify what exactly is being studied, how, compared to what, and measured by which outcome.

</details>

### Question 3 — Framework Application (Apply)
**[Short Answer]**

A PhD student in clinical research wants to study whether a new drug reduces blood pressure better than the current standard treatment in elderly patients. Which framework should they use, and how would they fill in its components?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

They should use **PICO**:
- **P** (Population): Elderly patients (age ≥ 65) with hypertension
- **I** (Intervention): The new drug (e.g., Drug X)
- **C** (Comparison): Current standard treatment (e.g., amlodipine)
- **O** (Outcome): Change in systolic blood pressure at 12 weeks

Research Question: "In elderly patients with hypertension, does Drug X reduce systolic blood pressure more effectively than amlodipine at 12 weeks?"

</details>

### Question 4 — Gap Analysis (Analyze)
**[Multiple Choice]**

According to this lesson, what is the "Future Work hack" for finding research gaps?

- A) Using AI to automatically generate research questions.
- B) Reading the "Future Work" or "Limitations" sections of the highest-PageRank papers in your citation network, because they are roadmaps written by the field's leading experts.
- C) Emailing professors at other universities to ask what they're working on.
- D) Searching for retracted papers and redoing the experiments.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — The "Future Work" sections of foundational papers are literal instructions from the top minds in the field about what still needs to be done. Combined with citation network analysis, this is the most systematic approach to gap identification.

</details>

---

### Question 5 — Evaluation (Evaluate)
**[Short Answer]**

A student proposes the following research question: "What is the effect of social media on teenager mental health across all countries, using every available dataset, measured by every possible psychological scale?"

Using the FINER criteria, identify which criterion this question fails most severely, and suggest how to fix it.

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

This question fails **Feasibility** most severely. "All countries," "every available dataset," and "every possible psychological scale" make it impossible to complete within a PhD timeline. 

Fix: Narrow the scope drastically. Example: "What is the effect of Instagram usage (>2 hours/day) on anxiety (measured by GAD-7) in UK adolescents aged 13–17?"

</details>
