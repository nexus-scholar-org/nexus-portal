# Module 5.1: Presenting at Conferences
**Series:** Modern Research Methodologies
**Module:** Sharing & Defending Your Research
**Tool:** None

---

## Scene 1: The Hook (≤ 30 seconds)

**[Visual]**
- A crowded conference hall. A student stands nervously next to a poster. The poster is a solid wall of 10-point text. People walk past without making eye contact.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"You've finally made it to a major conference. You spent weeks on your poster. You packed it with every method, every p-value, every nuance of your three-year project. 

And everyone is walking right past it.

Why? Because you designed a document, not a billboard. Today, we're going to fix your posters, fix your slides, and change how you think about academic conferences entirely."

---

## Scene 2: The Context (2–3 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- A funnel graphic. At the top: 1,000 attendees. Middle: 50 people at your talk. Bottom: 5 people who read your paper.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Let's clear up the biggest misconception about conferences. You are not there to teach the audience everything you did. You are there to advertise your paper. 

A conference presentation — whether a talk or a poster — is a trailer for the movie. If they want the full story, they can read the paper. Your only goal is to make them *want* to read the paper, or better yet, come talk to you about a collaboration."

---

## Scene 3: The Modern Poster (3–5 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- Side-by-side comparison:
  - Left: Traditional "Wall of Text" poster.
  - Right: "Better Poster" design (Huge central takeaway, QR code, clean visuals).

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"The traditional academic poster is fundamentally broken. It's impossible to read from five feet away.

Enter the 'Billboard' approach. 
1. The center of your poster should be one massive, plain-English sentence stating your main finding. 
2. Use a QR code that links directly to a PDF of your paper. 
3. Remove 80% of the text. Put your beautiful charts front and center.

Remember: The poster doesn't have to explain everything. *You* are standing right next to it. The poster is just a prop to start a conversation with you."

---

## Scene 4: The 10-Minute Talk (5–8 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- A timeline bar filling up: Hook (1m) → Context (2m) → Contribution (5m) → Takeaway (2m).

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"What if you're giving a 10-minute talk? Time is your enemy. Do not spend 4 minutes explaining standard methods that everyone in the room already knows. 

Use this structure:
**Minute 1 (The Hook):** State the big problem.
**Minutes 2-3 (The Context):** What's the specific gap you are addressing?
**Minutes 4-8 (The Contribution):** Show your results. Use high-contrast, simple graphs. No tables with 50 numbers.
**Minutes 9-10 (The Takeaway):** What is the one thing they should remember tomorrow?

And please, never turn your back to the audience to read your slides. If you have to read your slides, your slides have too much text."

---

## Scene 5: The Elevator Pitch (2 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- A timer counting down from 30 seconds. Bullet points: Who, Problem, Impact, Finding.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"The most important presentation you give at a conference won't be on a stage. It will be in the coffee line. You need a 30-second elevator pitch.

'Hi, I'm Sarah, a PhD student at MIT. I study battery degradation because current models underestimate failure rates by 20%. Recently, we found that modifying the electrolyte interface doubles the lifespan, which means we can make EVs significantly cheaper.'

Who you are. The problem. The impact. The finding. Thirty seconds."

---

## Scene 6: Summary (≤ 30 seconds)

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Let's recap:
1. **Your goal is advertising:** Conferences are for networking and getting your paper read.
2. **Posters are billboards:** Huge central takeaway, minimal text, QR code.
3. **Talks need structure:** Focus on the contribution and the takeaway; skip standard methods.
4. **Nail the elevator pitch:** Who, Problem, Impact, Finding."

---

## Scene 7: Standout Close

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Your research deserves to be seen. Stop hiding it behind walls of text."
