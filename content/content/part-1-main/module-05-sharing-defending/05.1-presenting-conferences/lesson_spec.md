## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:
1. **Design** an academic poster using the "Billboard" principle (less text, more visual hierarchy).
2. **Structure** a 10-minute conference talk to maximize audience retention (Hook, Context, Contribution, Takeaway).
3. **Deliver** an effective "elevator pitch" for their research.
4. **Evaluate** common presentation mistakes (reading off slides, laser-pointer dancing, cramming data).

---

## Key Concepts

### Concept 1: The Purpose of a Conference
- Conferences are not for presenting every data point you've collected. They are for **networking** and **advertising your paper**.
- Your goal is to get people to read your paper or collaborate with you, not to teach them everything you did in 10 minutes.

### Concept 2: The Modern Poster
- The traditional poster is a wall of text that nobody reads.
- **The "Better Poster" (Billboard) approach:** Large, central takeaway sentence. Minimal text. High-quality graphics. A QR code linking to the full paper.
- A poster is a prop for a conversation, not a standalone document.

### Concept 3: The 10-Minute Talk Structure
1. **The Hook (1 min):** Why does this matter?
2. **The Context (2 mins):** What is the gap in the literature?
3. **The Contribution (5 mins):** Your key findings and visuals. (Do not explain standard methods; focus on what's new).
4. **The Takeaway (2 mins):** What should the audience remember tomorrow?

### Concept 4: The Elevator Pitch
- A 30-second summary of who you are and what you do.
- "I'm [Name], a PhD student at [University]. I study [Problem] because [Impact]. Recently, we found [Key Finding], which means [Implication]."

---

## Suggested Hooks & Motivation
- **Pain Point:** You spent 40 hours designing a poster, and at the conference, people walk right past it while looking at their phones.
- **Wow Moment:** Transforming a dense, unreadable slide into a clean, punchy visual that immediately communicates the core finding.
