# Exercise: The Elevator Pitch and The Billboard
**Module:** 5.1 — Presenting at Conferences
**Estimated Time:** 20 minutes
**Difficulty:** ⭐⭐ Intermediate

---

## Scenario
> You have a major conference next month. You need to prepare both your 30-second elevator pitch for the coffee line and the design concept for your poster.

---

## Task 1: The Elevator Pitch
Draft your 30-second elevator pitch using the formula from the lesson. 

Fill in the blanks:
1. **Who you are:** "Hi, I'm [Name], a [Title] at [Institution]."
2. **The Problem & Impact:** "I study [X] because [Why it matters / The gap]."
3. **The Finding & Implication:** "Recently, we found [Y], which means [Z]."

*Read it out loud. Does it take longer than 30 seconds? If yes, cut words.*

---

## Task 2: The Billboard Poster Concept
Instead of writing 1,000 words for your poster, design the core visual hierarchy.

1. **The Hero Sentence:** Write the single, massive sentence that will go in the center of your poster. It must be easily readable from 10 feet away. (e.g., "Transformers outperform CNNs on Medical Images, but only when trained on multi-site data.")
2. **The Hero Graphic:** Describe the single most important chart or image you will put beneath that sentence. (e.g., "A bar chart comparing accuracy across 3 hospitals, showing the massive drop for the CNN.")
3. **The QR Code Link:** What exactly will you link your QR code to? (e.g., A preprint on arXiv, a GitHub repository with code, or a digital copy of the poster).

---

## Task 3: The 10-Minute Talk Structure
Map out your current or past research project to the 10-minute talk structure.

- **Min 1 (Hook):** [One sentence: Why do we care?]
- **Min 2-3 (Context):** [One sentence: What's the specific gap?]
- **Min 4-8 (Contribution):** [Two sentences: What did you find? Skip the methods.]
- **Min 9-10 (Takeaway):** [One sentence: What should they remember tomorrow?]

---

## Solution / Self-Check
<details>
<summary>Click to reveal the rubric</summary>

**Task 1:** If your pitch includes words like "utilizing," "methodological," or specific Greek letters, it's too dense. Use plain English. The coffee line is loud.
**Task 2:** If your Hero Sentence uses passive voice or hedges too much (e.g., "An investigation into the potential effects of..."), rewrite it as an active statement of your finding.
**Task 3:** The biggest mistake in Task 3 is sneaking methods into the Contribution section. Nobody cares what software version you used during a 10-minute talk. They only care about the results.
</details>
