# Module 5.3: Building Your Academic Profile
**Series:** Modern Research Methodologies
**Module:** Sharing & Defending Your Research

---

## Scene 1: The Hook (≤ 30 seconds)

**[Visual]**
- A search bar: "Jonathan A. Smith publications."
- Results show: J. Smith (physics), Jon Smith (biology), J. A. Smith (poetry), and J. Smith (a 19th-century historian).

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"You worked for three years on a paper. It finally gets published. A hiring committee searches for your name to see your work, but because the journal formatted your name as 'J. Smith,' your paper is buried under the work of a thousand other J. Smiths.

Your academic identity is fragile. If you don't take control of it, algorithms will mix you up, lose your citations, and hide your work. Today, we're building your digital footprint."

---

## Scene 2: The Foundation — ORCID & Google Scholar (2–3 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- The green ORCID logo. A unique 16-digit number links a researcher to their papers, grants, and peer reviews.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"The foundation of your academic profile is your ORCID iD. 

ORCID stands for Open Researcher and Contributor ID. It is a unique 16-digit number that acts as your academic social security number. If your name changes, if you move universities, or if a journal misspells your name, your ORCID ensures the paper remains attached to you forever. 

Once you have your ORCID, your next step is a Google Scholar profile. It takes five minutes to set up. It automatically tracks your citations and calculates your h-index. For most researchers, this will be the most frequently visited page of their entire career."

---

## Scene 3: The Academic Website (3–5 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- A wireframe of a clean, minimalist personal website.
- Highlights: Photo, Bio, Publications (with PDF links), CV.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"You need a personal website. Not a university directory page that disappears when you graduate, and not a massive, flashy portfolio. You need a digital business card.

Keep it simple. You only need four things:
1. **A professional photo and a two-sentence bio.** What do you study?
2. **Your Publications.** Include links to the open-access PDFs (Green OA) so people can actually read them.
3. **Your CV.** Available as a downloadable PDF.
4. **Contact info.** 

You can host this for free on GitHub Pages, use a Notion template, or build a simple site. Update it twice a year. That's it."

---

## Scene 4: Social Media — Signal vs. Noise (3–5 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- Icons for Twitter/X, LinkedIn, and ResearchGate.
- A thumb scrolling through a feed of new paper announcements.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"What about social media? 

Academic Twitter (or X) is the global watercooler. It's where you find out about papers months before they are formally published, hear about postdoc openings, and network with researchers you'd never meet otherwise. The key is to be a curator: share interesting papers, ask questions, and post a short thread when you publish a new paper. 

LinkedIn is essential if you have any interest in industry research or policy work.

ResearchGate and Academia.edu? Use them with caution. They are for-profit companies that spam your colleagues and often violate publisher copyrights. Your own website and Google Scholar are much safer bets."

---

## Scene 5: Summary (≤ 30 seconds)

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Let's recap:
1. **ORCID:** Claim your unique 16-digit identifier to secure your attribution.
2. **Google Scholar:** Set up your profile so others can find your work and citations.
3. **Personal Website:** Build a minimal digital business card with your bio, CV, and PDFs.
4. **Social Media:** Use it for networking and curation, not just broadcasting."

---

## Scene 6: Standout Close

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Make it as easy as possible for people to find you, read you, and cite you."
