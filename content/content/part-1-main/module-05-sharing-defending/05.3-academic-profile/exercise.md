# Exercise: The Profile Setup
**Module:** 5.3 — Building Your Academic Profile
**Estimated Time:** 15 minutes
**Difficulty:** ⭐ Beginner

---

## Scenario
> You are applying for a postdoc position. The hiring committee is going to Google your name. You have 15 minutes to ensure they find the right information.

---

## Tasks

### Task 1: Claim Your ORCID
If you do not have an ORCID iD, go to `orcid.org` and register for one right now. 
1. Record your 16-digit ORCID here: `____-____-____-____`
2. Add your current university affiliation to the profile.

### Task 2: Claim Your Google Scholar Profile
If you have at least one publication (even a preprint or a thesis in a university repository), go to Google Scholar and click "My Profile."
1. Ensure your affiliation is verified with a university email address.
2. Search for your name and "claim" your articles so they appear on your profile.
3. **Crucial step:** Make your profile "Public." (It is private by default).

### Task 3: The 2-Sentence Bio
Draft the exact 2-sentence bio you will put on your personal website or Twitter profile. 

*Formula:*
"I am a [Title/Position] at [Institution] researching [Broad Topic]. Specifically, I use [Method/Tool] to solve [Specific Problem]."

[Write here]

**Task 1:** Make sure to include your ORCID on every manuscript submission moving forward. Most submission systems now have a button for this.
**Task 2:** If you skip the step to make your Google Scholar profile public, no one will be able to see it, defeating the purpose entirely.
**Task 3:** Example bio: "I am a PhD candidate at MIT researching battery degradation in electric vehicles. Specifically, I use machine learning to predict electrolyte failure rates." Keep it simple and free of excessive jargon.
