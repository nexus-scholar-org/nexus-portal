### Q1 (Remember)
**[Multiple Choice]** What does the acronym FAIR stand for in data management?
- A) Free, Accurate, Integrated, Reproducible
- B) Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable
- C) Fast, Anonymous, Independent, Reliable
- D) Formatted, Analyzed, Interpreted, Reviewed

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B**</details>

---

### Q2 (Understand)
**[Multiple Choice]** Why is a Google Drive or Dropbox link not an acceptable way to share research data in a publication?
- A) Because they cost money.
- B) Because they do not provide a persistent Digital Object Identifier (DOI), and the links often break when the researcher changes institutions.
- C) Because they are blocked in Europe.
- D) Because they compress the data.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B** — Persistent repositories like Zenodo guarantee the data will remain available for decades.</details>

### Q3 (Apply)
**[Multiple Choice]** You are writing a Python script for your analysis. What is the most critical thing to include in your repository to ensure someone else can run it two years from now?
- A) A detailed PDF explaining your methodology.
- B) A `requirements.txt` or `environment.yml` file specifying the exact versions of the libraries you used.
- C) A copy of the Python installer.
- D) A disclaimer that the code might not work.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B** — Software libraries update constantly. Without knowing the exact versions you used, your code will likely break.</details>

---

### Q4 (Analyze)
**[Short Answer]** A researcher publishes a paper under "Green Open Access." What does this mean compared to "Gold Open Access"?

<details><summary>Sample Answer</summary>In Green OA, the author publishes in a subscription journal (behind a paywall) but self-archives the accepted manuscript in a free, public repository. In Gold OA, the author pays an Article Processing Charge (APC) to the journal to make the final publisher-formatted version free to everyone immediately.</details>

---

### Q5 (Evaluate)
**[Multiple Choice]** A researcher argues that they cannot share their data because it contains sensitive patient medical records. According to FAIR principles, what is the best approach?
- A) Do not share anything. Just put "Data not available" in the paper.
- B) Share the data anyway; science requires transparency.
- C) Do not share the raw data, but publish the *metadata* (describing the dataset) and share a synthetic or anonymized "toy" dataset so the code can still be tested.
- D) Post the data on the dark web.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**C** — Accessible does not mean "open to everyone." It means the conditions of access are clear, and the metadata is always available.</details>
