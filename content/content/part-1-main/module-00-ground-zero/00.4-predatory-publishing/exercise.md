# Exercise: The Predatory Audit
**Module:** 0.4 — Predatory Publishing
**Estimated Time:** 15 minutes
**Difficulty:** ⭐ Beginner
**Nexus Tool:** None (Web search)

---

## Scenario

> You recently presented a poster at a university symposium. Since then, your inbox has been flooded with emails from publishers. You need to publish to graduate, but you are terrified of accidentally submitting to a predatory journal and ruining your CV.
> 
> Act as an academic detective to audit the following three email invitations.

---

## Tasks

### Task 1: Audit Email A
**The Email:**
*Subject: Urgent: Invitation to Publish in American Journal of Advanced Medical Research*
*Dear Esteemed Colleague,*
*We read with great interest your recent poster presentation on campus. The American Journal of Advanced Medical Research (Impact Factor: 14.5) cordially invites you to submit a full research paper. We offer a rapid 48-hour peer-review track to guarantee publication in our upcoming September issue. Please submit your manuscript by Friday with the $800 processing fee.*

**Your Job:**
1. List all the red flags in this email.
2. Would you submit to this journal?

---

### Task 2: Audit Email B
**The Email:**
*Subject: Open Access Publication in PLOS ONE*
*Dear Dr. Smith,*
*This is an automated notification from the Editorial Board of PLOS ONE. Your submitted manuscript "Effects of Urban Noise on Avian Migration" has completed its initial desk review and has been sent to three independent reviewers. Our average time to first decision is currently 42 days. If accepted, our standard Article Processing Charge (APC) of $2,100 will apply, though fee waivers are available for researchers from low-income countries.*

**Your Job:**
1. This journal charges a massive $2,100 fee. Does this mean it is predatory? Why or why not?
2. What details in this email suggest it is a legitimate process?

---

### Task 3: The Whitelist Verification
**Scenario:**
You want to submit to the *Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research (JAIR)*. It claims to be an open-access journal. 

**Your Job:**
1. Go to the Directory of Open Access Journals (https://doaj.org).
2. Search for the *Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research*.
3. Is it listed? What does this tell you about its legitimacy?

### Task 1 Solution
1. **Red Flags:**
   - Unsolicited, overly flattering spam email ("Dear Esteemed Colleague").
   - "Rapid 48-hour peer-review". Real peer review takes weeks or months. 48 hours is physically impossible for rigorous review.
   - Demanding the fee upfront with submission rather than upon acceptance.
   - The title is highly generic ("Advanced Medical Research") designed to cast a wide net.
2. **Verdict:** Absolutely not. This is a classic predatory scam.

### Task 2 Solution
1. **The Fee:** No, charging an APC does not make a journal predatory. PLOS ONE is a highly respected, legitimate Open Access journal. The fee is to cover the publishing costs since they do not charge readers subscription fees.
2. **Green Flags:** 
   - They specify "three independent reviewers."
   - The timeline is realistic ("average time to first decision is currently 42 days").
   - They offer fee waivers for researchers who cannot afford it, showing their primary goal is disseminating science, not just profit.

### Task 3 Solution
1. Yes, the *Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research* is listed in the DOAJ. 
2. Because it is on the definitive whitelist, you can be highly confident that it employs rigorous, legitimate peer review and is a safe venue for your manuscript. (In fact, JAIR is one of the premier journals in AI).
