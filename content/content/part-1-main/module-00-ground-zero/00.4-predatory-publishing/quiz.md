# Quiz: Module 0.4 — Predatory Publishing

**Instructions:** Answer all questions to test your ability to spot and avoid predatory academic publishers.

---

### Question 1 — Conceptual (Remember)
**[Multiple Choice]**

What is the primary defining characteristic of a **predatory journal**?

- A) They charge authors a fee to publish their work.
- B) They only publish their articles on the internet, not in print.
- C) They charge publication fees but provide no legitimate peer review or editorial oversight.
- D) They reject more than 90% of the papers submitted to them.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C** — The defining characteristic of a predatory journal is the lack of quality control (peer review). Charging a fee (APC) is standard practice for legitimate Open Access journals, but legitimate journals actually perform the rigorous review they claim to.

</details>

---

### Question 2 — Conceptual (Understand)
**[Multiple Choice]**

Why do legitimate Open Access (OA) journals, such as *Nature Communications*, charge authors an Article Processing Charge (APC)?

- A) Because the peer reviewers demand a high salary.
- B) Because they do not charge readers or libraries subscription fees, so the cost of publishing must be shifted to the author.
- C) Because it proves the author's university is well-funded.
- D) To artificially lower the number of submissions they receive.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — The core of the Open Access model is that science should be free to read. Without subscription revenue, the costs of managing peer review, copyediting, and hosting are paid upfront by the author (usually via their grant funding) through the APC.

</details>

### Question 3 — Scenario-Based (Apply)
**[Multiple Choice]**

You receive an email inviting you to submit to the *International Journal of Global Science*. The email promises that your paper will be peer-reviewed and published online within **5 days**. What should you do?

- A) Submit immediately, as fast publications are best for your CV.
- B) Reply and negotiate the publication fee down.
- C) Delete the email. Legitimate peer review takes weeks or months; a 5-day guarantee is a massive red flag for a predatory journal.
- D) Ask your supervisor to be a co-author to lend credibility to the fast publication.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C** — Finding reviewers, having them read the paper, writing reports, and making an editorial decision is a slow process. Any promise of publication in a few days is a guarantee that the paper will not actually be reviewed.

</details>

---

### Question 4 — Conceptual (Apply)
**[Short Answer]**

What is the DOAJ, and how should a researcher use it to protect themselves?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

The DOAJ is the Directory of Open Access Journals. It is the definitive "whitelist" of legitimate OA journals that adhere to high standards of peer review. A researcher should search the DOAJ before submitting a manuscript; if an OA journal is not listed there, it should be treated with extreme suspicion.

</details>

### Question 5 — Scenario-Based (Evaluate)
**[Multiple Choice]**

A junior researcher knowingly publishes a mediocre paper in a predatory journal just to increase their total publication count before applying for a tenure-track faculty job. Why is this a disastrous career move?

- A) Because hiring committees will see the predatory journal on the CV, recognize the lack of academic integrity or judgment, and penalize the candidate.
- B) Because the researcher will not receive any royalties from the predatory publisher.
- C) Because predatory journals usually fix formatting errors, making the researcher look lazy.
- D) It's actually a good move, as hiring committees only look at the total number of papers.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A** — Academic hiring committees are deeply familiar with predatory journals. Listing a predatory publication on a CV is a massive red flag that signals poor judgment or an attempt to cheat the system, often leading to immediate rejection.

</details>
