# Module 0.1: Anatomy of a Research Paper
**Series:** Modern Research Methodologies
**Module:** Ground Zero
**Tool:** None (Conceptual)

---

## Scene 1: The Hook (≤ 30 seconds)

**[Visual]**
- A student sitting at a desk surrounded by tall stacks of printed papers, looking overwhelmed.
- Close up on a dense, text-heavy page of a research paper.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Have you ever sat down with a 20-page research paper, started reading at the Introduction, and by page four you realize you have no idea what they're talking about? You're exhausted, you've wasted thirty minutes, and you still don't know if this paper is actually useful for your thesis. 

If this sounds familiar, you're not alone. The problem isn't that you aren't smart enough. The problem is that you are reading the paper like it's a novel. Today, I'm going to teach you a superpower: how to read a paper like a scientist."

---

## Scene 2: The Context (2–3 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- Slide showing an encyclopedia index vs. a novel.
- Slide introducing the IMRAD acronym.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Novices read papers linearly, from start to finish. This is incredibly inefficient. Experts read strategically. You don't read an encyclopedia from A to Z; you use the index to find exactly what you need. A research paper should be treated the exact same way.

To do this, you first have to understand how papers are built. Since the mid-20th century, the vast majority of empirical research papers follow a standardized blueprint known as IMRAD: Introduction, Methods, Results, And Discussion.

Think of IMRAD like the blueprint of a house. The Introduction is the porch—it welcomes you and sets the context. The Methods are the foundation—how the house was built. The Results are the rooms—what is actually inside. And the Discussion is the neighborhood—how this house fits in with all the others on the street."

---

## Scene 3: The Concept (3–5 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- Breakdown of the 3-Pass Approach (Keshav, 2007).
- Visual timeline showing time spent on each pass.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Once you understand the blueprint, you can stop reading linearly. Instead, use what computer scientist S. Keshav calls the 'Three-Pass Approach.'

**Pass 1: The Triage.**
This takes 5 to 10 minutes. You read the Title, the Abstract, and the Introduction. You skip the Methods entirely. You glance at the section headings, and you read the Conclusion. The goal here isn't to understand the science deeply. The goal is simply to answer one question: *Is this paper relevant to my work?* If the answer is no, you drop it and move on.

**Pass 2: The Core.**
If the paper survives Pass 1, you spend about an hour on Pass 2. Here, you look closely at the Figures and Tables. In science, the figures *are* the paper. Then you read the Results and the Discussion. The goal here is to grasp the main findings and the overarching methodology without getting bogged down in the mathematical proofs or deep technical weeds.

**Pass 3: The Deep Dive.**
You only do this for the absolute most important papers in your field. This can take 4 to 5 hours. You read the Methods section line by line. Your goal is to virtually re-implement the paper in your head. Could you replicate this experiment? Are there flaws in their assumptions?"

## Scene 4: The Demonstration (5–8 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- Screencast of a PDF viewer opening a real scientific paper (e.g., the original Attention Is All You Need paper or a classic biology paper).
- Highlighting sections as the voiceover mentions them.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Let's look at a real paper. Notice the Abstract—it's exactly one paragraph. It contains a mini-version of the entire IMRAD structure: background, what they did, what they found, and why it matters.

Now, I'm doing Pass 1. I read the Abstract. I scroll down... I'm completely ignoring this wall of text in the Methods section. Instead, I jump straight to the end of the Introduction, where the authors explicitly state their contributions: *'In this paper, we demonstrate X.'*

Okay, the paper is relevant. Let's start Pass 2. I jump to Figure 1. I read the caption carefully. I look at the axes of the graphs in the Results section. I can see the trend they are claiming. 

If I needed to replicate this, I would finally read the Methods section. Notice how we extracted the value of the paper in two minutes without reading the hardest parts?"

## Scene 5: Exercise Teaser (≤ 30 seconds)

**[Visual]**
- Title card: "Exercise: The 10-Minute Triage"

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"In the exercise for this lesson, you are going to practice Pass 1. You'll be given a link to a dense, complex research paper. Set a timer for 10 minutes, and answer three specific questions about it without reading the Methods or Results text. It will feel unnatural at first, but it will save you hundreds of hours in your career."

---

## Scene 6: Summary (≤ 30 seconds)

**[Visual]**
- Summary slide with 3 bullet points.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Let's recap:
1. **IMRAD** is the blueprint: Introduction, Methods, Results, And Discussion.
2. **Stop reading linearly.** Treat a paper like an encyclopedia, not a novel.
3. **Use the Three-Pass Approach:** Triage in 10 minutes, grasp the core in an hour, and only deep-dive into the methods when absolutely necessary."
