### Question 1 — Conceptual (Remember)
**[Multiple Choice]**

What does the acronym IMRAD stand for in academic publishing?

- A) Introduction, Methodology, Research, And Data
- B) Information, Methods, Results, And Discussion
- C) Introduction, Methods, Results, And Discussion
- D) Inquiry, Metrics, Review, And Discovery

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C** — Introduction, Methods, Results, And Discussion. This has been the standard blueprint for empirical research papers since the mid-20th century.

</details>

---

### Question 2 — Conceptual (Understand)
**[Multiple Choice]**

According to the Three-Pass Approach (Keshav, 2007), what is the primary goal of "Pass 1"?

- A) To deeply understand the statistical methods used.
- B) To virtually re-implement the experiment.
- C) To quickly triage the paper and decide if it is relevant enough to warrant further reading.
- D) To analyze the trends shown in the figures and tables.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C** — Pass 1 (Triage) takes 5-10 minutes and focuses on the title, abstract, intro, and conclusion to simply determine if the paper is worth your time.

</details>

---

### Question 3 — Scenario-Based (Apply)
**[Multiple Choice]**

You are conducting a Pass 2 scan of a paper. Which of the following components should you focus on heavily during this pass?

- A) The mathematical proofs in the appendix.
- B) The Figures, Tables, and their captions.
- C) The exact brand of laboratory equipment used.
- D) The historical background in the first paragraph of the introduction.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — Pass 2 focuses heavily on the visual data (Figures and Tables) and the Results/Discussion to grasp the core findings without getting bogged down in the minute methodological details.

</details>

---

### Question 4 — Conceptual (Analyze)
**[Short Answer]**

Why is it dangerous to treat a "Preprint" (e.g., from arXiv) with the same level of trust as a paper published in a standard journal?

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

Preprints have **not been peer-reviewed**. While they are useful for accessing cutting-edge research quickly, they have not undergone independent scrutiny by other scientists in the field. Therefore, the methods and conclusions must be evaluated with a much higher degree of critical skepticism by the reader.

</details>

---

### Question 5 — Analogy (Understand)
**[Multiple Choice]**

Using the "house blueprint" analogy for IMRAD, which section is represented by "The Neighborhood" (how this specific house fits in with the other houses on the street)?

- A) Introduction
- B) Methods
- C) Results
- D) Discussion

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D** — The Discussion section takes the specific results of the paper and contextualizes them within the broader scientific literature (the neighborhood), explaining why these results matter to the field as a whole.

</details>
