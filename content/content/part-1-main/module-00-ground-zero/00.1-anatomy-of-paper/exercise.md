# Exercise: The 10-Minute Triage
**Module:** 0.1 — Anatomy of a Research Paper
**Estimated Time:** 20 minutes
**Difficulty:** ⭐ Beginner
**Nexus Tool:** None

---

## Scenario

> You are doing a literature review and you have 30 papers sitting in your "To Read" folder. If you read them cover-to-cover, it will take you a month. Your goal today is to practice the **Pass 1 Triage** on a real, dense scientific paper. 

---

## Tasks

### Task 1: Find a Target Paper
**Objective:** Select a primary research article to practice on.

1. Go to an open-access repository like arXiv, PubMed Central, or PLOS ONE.
2. Search for a topic you are interested in (e.g., "climate change impacts on agriculture", "transformer neural networks", "CRISPR gene editing").
3. Download the PDF of a **primary research article** (not a review article). Ensure it is at least 10 pages long.

---

### Task 2: The 10-Minute Triage (Pass 1)
**Objective:** Extract the core information without reading linearly.

**Instructions:**
1. Set a physical timer or phone timer for exactly **10 minutes**.
2. Start the timer. 
3. **DO NOT read the Methods or Results sections.**
4. Read only the following:
   - The Title
   - The Abstract
   - The last two paragraphs of the Introduction
   - The Conclusion (or Discussion summary)
   - Glance at the section headings.
5. Answer the following questions before the timer goes off:

**Questions to answer in 10 minutes:**
- Q1. What is the specific problem the authors are trying to solve?
- Q2. What is their main proposed solution or primary finding?
- Q3. Is this paper highly relevant, somewhat relevant, or completely irrelevant to your hypothetical thesis topic?

---

### Task 3: The Visual Scan (Pass 2 Preview)
**Objective:** Extract findings purely from figures and tables.

**Instructions:**
1. Take an additional **5 minutes**.
2. Look *only* at the Figures and Tables in the paper. 
3. Read the captions below the figures carefully.

**Questions to answer in 5 minutes:**
- Q4. Without reading the text, can you identify the most important graph or table in the paper? What trend or result does it show?

---

## Reflection

How did it feel to explicitly skip over dense text? Were you able to answer the core questions about the paper without reading the Methods section? 

Most students find this highly uncomfortable at first—they feel like they are "cheating." But remember: this is exactly how senior professors and researchers process literature.

---

## Solution / Self-Check

<details>
<summary>Click to view self-evaluation criteria</summary>

Because you chose your own paper, there is no single "correct" answer, but evaluate your performance against these criteria:

- **Timing:** Did you actually stop at 10 minutes? If you ran out of time, you were likely reading too deeply. Next time, skim faster.
- **Q1 (Problem):** You should have found this easily in the Abstract or the first paragraph of the Introduction.
- **Q2 (Solution):** This is almost always stated explicitly in the final paragraph of the Introduction (e.g., "In this paper, we propose...") and reiterated in the Conclusion.
- **Q4 (Figures):** Well-written scientific papers are designed so that the figures and captions tell the entire story on their own. If you couldn't understand the figure, it might be a poorly written paper!
