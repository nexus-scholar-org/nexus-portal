# Quiz: Module 0.2 — Research Methodologies

**Instructions:** Answer all questions to test your understanding of quantitative, qualitative, and mixed methods research.

### Question 1 — Conceptual (Remember)
**[Multiple Choice]**

Which of the following best describes **Quantitative** research?

- A) It relies on inductive reasoning to build new theories.
- B) It focuses on subjective human experiences and meaning.
- C) It relies on deductive reasoning, objective measurement, and statistical analysis.
- D) It prioritizes data saturation over statistical power.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C** — Quantitative research is the language of numbers, utilizing deductive logic to test existing theories through objective measurement and statistics.

</details>

---

### Question 2 — Conceptual (Understand)
**[Multiple Choice]**

What is the primary factor that determines whether a researcher achieves an adequate sample size in a **Qualitative** interview study?

- A) Calculating statistical power to ensure a p-value < 0.05.
- B) Reaching "data saturation," where new interviews no longer yield new themes or insights.
- C) Reaching a minimum threshold of 100 participants.
- D) Matching the sample size to the nearest quantitative census data.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — Qualitative research does not use statistical power. Instead, researchers use "data saturation," stopping data collection when no new information or themes are being discovered in the interviews.

</details>

---

### Question 3 — Scenario-Based (Apply)
**[Multiple Choice]**

A researcher wants to know: *"How do first-generation college students experience imposter syndrome during their freshman year?"* Which methodology is most appropriate?

- A) A randomized controlled trial (RCT) testing a new study technique.
- B) An A/B test of two different campus navigation apps.
- C) A phenomenological qualitative study using in-depth, semi-structured interviews.
- D) A quantitative survey asking students to rate their stress from 1 to 5.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C** — The question asks "How do they experience...", which requires exploring subjective meaning and deep context. This is the domain of qualitative research.

</details>

---

### Question 4 — Conceptual (Analyze)
**[Short Answer]**

Explain the difference between Reliability and Validity using the analogy of a bathroom scale.

<details>
<summary>Sample Answer</summary>

Reliability refers to consistency: if you step on the scale five times in a row, does it give you the exact same number every time? 
Validity refers to accuracy: is the number the scale gives you actually your true weight?
A broken scale that always reads exactly 150 lbs, regardless of who steps on it, is perfectly reliable (consistent) but completely invalid (inaccurate).

</details>

---

### Question 5 — Scenario-Based (Evaluate)
**[Multiple Choice]**

A tech company runs a large-scale A/B test on their website and proves conclusively that a red "Buy" button increases sales by 12% compared to a blue button. However, the executives want to know *why* the red button works better. What should the research team do?

- A) Run the A/B test again with a larger sample size to increase reliability.
- B) Switch to a Mixed Methods approach by conducting qualitative user interviews to understand the psychological reasoning behind the users' clicks.
- C) Assume the red button creates a sense of urgency, based on deductive logic.
- D) Run a new quantitative survey asking users to rate the color red on a scale of 1 to 10.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B** — The quantitative A/B test answered "Does X cause Y?" (Yes, red causes more clicks). To answer the "Why" question, the team needs qualitative context, making a Mixed Methods approach ideal.

</details>
