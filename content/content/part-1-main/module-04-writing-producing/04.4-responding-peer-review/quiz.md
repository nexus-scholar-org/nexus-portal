# Quiz: Module 4.4 — Responding to Peer Review

---

### Q1 (Remember)
**[MC]** What is the recommended format for a revision response letter?
- A) A general summary of all changes made
- B) Point-by-point: quote each comment, respond directly, show the change
- C) A letter thanking the editor and ignoring minor comments
- D) A rebuttal arguing why the reviewers are wrong

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B**</details>

---

### Q2 (Understand)
**[MC]** A reviewer writes: "I disagree with the authors' interpretation of the results." What is the best response strategy?
- A) Argue that the reviewer is wrong and cite additional evidence.
- B) Acknowledge the alternative interpretation, add a paragraph in the Discussion presenting both views, and thank the reviewer.
- C) Ignore the comment and hope the editor doesn't notice.
- D) Withdraw the paper.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B** — You don't have to agree, but you must demonstrate you considered their view.</details>

---

### Q3 (Apply)
**[Short Answer]** Rewrite this defensive response professionally: "The reviewer clearly did not read our paper carefully. We already addressed this in Section 3."

<details><summary>Sample Answer</summary>"We thank the reviewer for raising this point. We addressed this issue in Section 3 (p. 5, lines 98–105). To improve clarity, we have added a cross-reference in Section 1 directing the reader to this discussion (p. 2, line 34)."</details>

---

### Q4 (Analyze)
**[MC]** Why is a "Major Revisions" decision generally considered a positive outcome?
- A) Because the editor liked the paper's formatting.
- B) Because fewer than 3% of papers are accepted outright, so Major Revisions means the editor sees publication potential and is giving you a chance to improve.
- C) Because it means the paper will be automatically published after changes.
- D) Because it means the reviewers didn't find any problems.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B**</details>

---

### Q5 (Evaluate)
**[MC]** After receiving a rejection, what is the recommended next step?
- A) Give up on the paper.
- B) Resubmit the identical paper to a different journal without changes.
- C) Read the reviews for feedback, revise the paper accordingly, identify a more appropriate journal, and resubmit.
- D) Email the editor to argue against the decision.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**C**</details>
