# Module 4.2: Writing with AI: Ethics & Best Practices
**Series:** Modern Research Methodologies
**Module:** Writing & Producing Research
**Tool:** None

## Scene 1: The Hook (≤ 30 seconds)

**[Visual]**
- Screen recording of a user typing into ChatGPT: "Write a 500-word introduction about the history of quantum computing with citations."
- The AI rapidly generates text. The user copies and pastes it into Word.
- A red stamp slams down: "ACADEMIC MISCONDUCT."

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"It's 2 AM, your deadline is in eight hours, and the blank page is staring at you. The temptation to ask an AI to just 'write the introduction' is overwhelming. 

Don't do it. Not just because it's unethical, but because it will produce generic, hallucinated garbage. Today, we're going to learn how to use AI the right way — not as a ghostwriter, but as a structural editor and thought partner."

## Scene 2: The Context (2–3 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- Side-by-side comparison:
  - Left: "Ghostwriter" (Unethical, generates content).
  - Right: "Copilot" (Ethical, refines structure).

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"The landscape of academic writing changed overnight with the release of Large Language Models. Every major publisher — Nature, Science, IEEE — had to rapidly write new policies. 

The consensus across almost all disciplines is this: AI cannot be listed as an author, because an AI cannot take moral or legal responsibility for the work. You are 100% accountable for every word, data point, and citation in your paper. 

If you use AI to generate data or write original text, that is falsification and plagiarism. But if you use AI to improve the clarity of your own ideas, check your grammar, or restructure a messy paragraph? That is not only ethical — it's highly encouraged, especially for non-native English speakers."

---

## Scene 3: The Concept — Hallucinations (3–5 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- An AI-generated reference list. A magnifying glass hovers over a citation. A big red "X" appears next to it. "DOI NOT FOUND."

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Let's talk about the biggest danger: Hallucinations.

LLMs are not databases. They are statistical prediction engines. If you ask an LLM for a citation, it doesn't look it up; it predicts what a citation *should* look like based on patterns. It will invent authors who work in the field, attach them to a plausible-sounding title, and generate a fake DOI. 

It looks perfect. It is completely fake.

Never, ever ask an AI to find literature for you. Use the Nexus toolkit to find real papers. Only use AI to analyze the text you provide to it."

---

## Scene 4: The Demonstration — Prompting for Structure (5–8 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- Screen recording showing effective prompts.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"So how do we use it ethically? We prompt for structure, not content. 

**Bad Prompt:** 'Write a conclusion for my paper.'
**Good Prompt:** 'Here is my draft conclusion. Please check if it aligns with the IMRaD format. Point out any sentences that are overly complex, and suggest edits using the Paramedic Method to keep sentences under 25 words.'

**Bad Prompt:** 'Summarize the literature on this topic.'
**Good Prompt:** 'Here are three paragraphs I wrote synthesizing the literature. Can you identify if I used the "Convergence" or "Divergence" moves effectively? Are the transitions between paragraphs logical?'

When you prompt like this, the AI acts as a peer reviewer, not an author. The ideas remain yours. The structure is just polished."

---

## Scene 5: Transparency and Disclosure (2 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- A manuscript highlighting the "Acknowledgments" or "Methods" section, showing an AI disclosure statement.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Finally, if you use AI to assist in drafting, you must disclose it. 

Most journals require a simple statement like: *'During the preparation of this work, the author used [Tool Name] to [Reason, e.g., improve readability and grammar]. After using this tool, the author reviewed and edited the content as needed and takes full responsibility for the publication.'*

Transparency protects you."

---

## Scene 6: Summary (≤ 30 seconds)

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Let's recap:
1. **Copilot, not Autopilot:** Use AI to edit and structure your ideas, not to generate new ones.
2. **Accountability:** You are entirely responsible for the final output. AI cannot be an author.
3. **Beware Hallucinations:** Never use AI to find citations.
4. **Disclose:** Always declare your use of AI in your manuscript."
