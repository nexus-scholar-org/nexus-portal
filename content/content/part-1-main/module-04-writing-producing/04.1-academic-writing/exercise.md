# Exercise: The Reverse Outline
**Module:** 4.1 — Academic Writing: Structure & Style
**Estimated Time:** 25 minutes
**Difficulty:** ⭐⭐ Intermediate

---

## Scenario
> Take any well-written published paper in your field (ideally one you've already read in previous exercises).

## Tasks

### Task 1: Paragraph-Level Reverse Outline
For each paragraph in the Introduction, write one sentence describing what that paragraph *does* (its structural function), not what it *says* (its content).

Example:
| Paragraph | Function |
|:---|:---|
| Intro ¶1 | Establishes the broad significance of the field. |
| Intro ¶2 | Narrows to the specific sub-problem. |
| Intro ¶3 | Identifies the gap in the literature. |
| Intro ¶4 | States the research question and previews the paper's contribution. |

**Question 1:** Does the Introduction follow the funnel structure (broad → narrow → gap → question)?

### Task 2: Check the Methods
Scan the Methods section. Could you replicate this study from the description alone?

**Question 2:** List any missing details that would prevent replication.

### Task 3: Apply the Paramedic Method
Find the 3 longest sentences in the paper. For each one:
1. Underline the core subject and verb.
2. Delete or move everything non-essential.
3. Rewrite in under 20 words.

**Question 3:** How much shorter are your versions? Was any meaning lost?

---

## Solution / Self-Check
<details>
<summary>Click to reveal</summary>

### Task 1: If the Introduction doesn't follow broad → narrow → gap → question, it's poorly structured (common in older papers).
### Task 2: Missing details = the paper has a replicability problem. This is a known crisis in many fields.
### Task 3: If you cut 50%+ of a sentence without losing meaning, the original was overwritten. Academic writing rewards clarity, not complexity.
