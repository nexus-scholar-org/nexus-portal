## Scene 2: The Context (2–3 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- The IMRaD structure displayed as 4 blocks: Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Almost every research paper in the sciences follows a structure called IMRaD — Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion. This isn't a suggestion. It's the expected format for nearly every journal.

Understanding this structure means you never have to stare at a blank page again, because each section has a specific job, a specific question it answers, and a specific order in which to write it."

## Scene 3: The Concept — Section by Section (5–8 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- Each section gets its own card with the question it answers and writing tips.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Let me give you the blueprint for each section.

**Introduction.** This answers: 'Why should anyone care?' It follows a funnel structure. Start broad — the general field. Narrow to the specific problem. Identify the gap. State your research question. End with a roadmap of the paper.

**Methods.** This answers: 'How did you do it?' Write this section first. It's the easiest because you already know what you did. Be specific enough that someone could replicate your study.

**Results.** This answers: 'What did you find?' Report the data objectively. No interpretation. Use tables and figures. Let the numbers speak.

**Discussion.** This answers: 'What does it mean?' Interpret your results. Compare them to previous work. Acknowledge limitations honestly. Suggest future directions.

The secret? Don't write in order. Write Methods first, then Results, then Discussion, then Introduction last. The Introduction is the hardest because it requires you to know the full story — so write it after you've told the story."

## Scene 4: The Demonstration — Writing Mechanics (3–5 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- Side-by-side comparisons of bad vs. good academic writing.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Beyond structure, let's fix some common writing mistakes.

**Hedging Language.** Never write 'This proves that X.' Science doesn't prove; it provides evidence. Write: 'These results suggest that X' or 'The data are consistent with X.'

**Active vs. Passive Voice.** Old advice said always use passive: 'The experiment was conducted.' Modern journals prefer active voice: 'We conducted the experiment.' It's clearer, shorter, and more honest.

**Signposting.** Guide your reader with transition sentences. 'Having established the theoretical framework, we now describe the experimental design.' This sentence does nothing scientifically, but it does everything structurally.

**The Paramedic Method.** If a sentence is longer than 25 words, it's probably too long. Circle the core subject and verb. Delete everything that isn't essential."

## Scene 6: Summary (≤ 30 seconds)

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Let's recap:
1. **IMRaD:** Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion. Each section answers a specific question.
2. **Write out of order:** Methods → Results → Discussion → Introduction.
