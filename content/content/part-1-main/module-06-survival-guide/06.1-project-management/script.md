# Module 6.1: Project Management for Researchers
**Series:** Modern Research Methodologies
**Module:** The PhD Survival Guide

---

## Scene 1: The Hook (≤ 30 seconds)

**[Visual]**
- A student sitting at a desk, looking at a massive, intimidating mountain labeled "THE THESIS." They look stressed, open a new tab, and start scrolling social media.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"It's Monday morning. You sit down at your desk and look at your to-do list. There is only one item on it: 'Write Thesis.' 

It is so overwhelming, so massive, and so unstructured, that your brain panics and seeks immediate dopamine. So you check your email. You read the news. The day ends, and the thesis hasn't moved. 

A PhD is a five-year project with almost no intermediate deadlines. If you don't build your own structure, you will drown. Today, we're importing Project Management into academia."

---

## Scene 2: The Agile Academic (3–5 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- A Kanban board with sticky notes moving from left to right.

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"In software engineering, they realized long ago that multi-year projects fail if they aren't broken down. They invented Agile methodologies. We are going to steal them.

Meet the Academic Kanban board. You have five columns: Backlog, To Do, Doing, Waiting, and Done. 

**Backlog** is where good ideas go to wait.
**To Do** is what you commit to finishing in the next two weeks. 
**Doing** is what you are working on *today*. Never put more than two things in this column.
**Waiting** is crucial: this is for papers waiting on peer review, or code running on a cluster. 

This simple board takes the anxiety out of the thesis, because you no longer have to worry about the whole mountain. You only worry about the two sticky notes in the 'Doing' column."

---

## Scene 3: Managing Up (3–5 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- Split screen: A bad advisor meeting (unstructured, confused) vs. a good one (structured agenda).

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Next: Project managing your advisor. 

Your advisor is brilliant, but they are managing 10 other students, teaching classes, and writing grants. If you go to a meeting and say 'I don't know what to do,' they can't help you effectively.

You must 'manage up.' Never go to a meeting empty-handed. Bring a one-page agenda:
1. What I accomplished since last time.
2. The specific roadblock I hit.
3. My proposed solution.
4. Exactly what I need from you today.

You are the CEO of your project. Your advisor is the board of directors. Treat the meetings accordingly."

---

## Scene 4: Personal Knowledge Management (2–4 minutes)

**[Visual]**
- A digital brain with nodes lighting up and connecting (Zettelkasten visualization).

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Finally, you are processing a massive amount of information. You cannot hold four years of literature in your head. 

You need a Personal Knowledge Management system. The most popular among researchers is the Zettelkasten, or 'slip-box' method. You take atomic notes — one idea per note — and link them together in software like Obsidian or Roam. 

When you read a paper in Year 1, you write a note. In Year 4, when you are writing your thesis introduction, you don't start from a blank page. You start by assembling the notes you've been taking for three years."

---

## Scene 5: Summary (≤ 30 seconds)

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"Let's recap:
1. **Kanban Boards:** Break the thesis down into sprints. Focus only on the 'Doing' column.
2. **Manage Up:** Treat your advisor like a board of directors. Bring structured agendas.
3. **PKM:** Build a system to store and link your knowledge outside of your brain so your future self can use it."

---

## Scene 6: Standout Close

**[Audio / Voiceover]**
"You don't rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems."
