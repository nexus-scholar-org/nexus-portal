# Quiz: Module 6.3 — Mental Health & Imposter Syndrome

---

### Q1 (Remember)
**[Multiple Choice]** Which of the following best defines "Imposter Syndrome" in an academic context?
- A) Plagiarizing someone else's work.
- B) The feeling that you are a fraud, that you were admitted by mistake, and that you do not belong.
- C) Faking data in a laboratory experiment.
- D) Pretending to know how to code when you don't.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B**</details>

---

### Q2 (Understand)
**[Multiple Choice]** According to the Dunning-Kruger effect, what does a sudden drop in confidence during Year 3 of a PhD usually signify?
- A) That you have chosen the wrong research topic.
- B) That you are actually getting smarter and finally realizing how vast and complex the literature truly is.
- C) That your advisor is failing you.
- D) That you should drop out immediately.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B** — Recognizing the vastness of the unknown is a sign of increasing competence.</details>

---

### Q3 (Apply)
**[Multiple Choice]** You receive a desk rejection from a top journal. What is the healthiest way to process this event?
- A) Conclude that your research is worthless and abandon the project.
- B) Reply to the editor and demand they accept it.
- C) Treat the rejection as data, recognize that 90% of papers submitted there are rejected, and prepare to submit to your backup journal.
- D) Wait six months before looking at the paper again out of anxiety.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**C**</details>

---

### Q4 (Analyze)
**[Short Answer]** Explain what it means to "de-couple your identity" from your academic output, and why it is important for surviving a PhD.

<details><summary>Sample Answer</summary>De-coupling your identity means separating your self-worth as a human being from your success as a researcher. ("I do research," not "I am a researcher"). It is vital because academia is filled with rejection and failure; if your entire identity is tied to acceptances, you will suffer severe burnout.</details>

### Q5 (Evaluate)
**[Multiple Choice]** Why does the lesson argue that "feeling stupid" is a structural feature of doing a PhD?
- A) Because the admissions process is flawed.
- B) Because if you already knew the answers, it wouldn't be called "research." You are supposed to be looking at the unknown.
- C) Because professors deliberately give you impossible tasks.
- D) Because modern science is too hard for one person to understand.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B**</details>
