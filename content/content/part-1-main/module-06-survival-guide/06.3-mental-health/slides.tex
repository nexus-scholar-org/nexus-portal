\begin{frame}{What You'll Learn}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Recognize} Imposter Syndrome as a structural feature of academia.
    \item \textbf{Map} the Dunning-Kruger effect to the PhD timeline.
    \item \textbf{Reframe} rejection as data, not failure.
    \item \textbf{De-couple} personal identity from academic output.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The Imposter Phenomenon}
  \begin{alertblock}{The Feeling}
    ``They made a mistake letting me in. I don't belong here, and soon everyone will find out I am a fraud.''
  \end{alertblock}
  \vspace{0.3cm}
  \begin{exampleblock}{The Reality}
    Over 70\% of high-achievers experience this. It occurs because you are suddenly surrounded by the top 1\% of your field, and the very nature of research is staring at the ``unknown.'' Feeling stupid is evidence you are doing actual research.
  \end{exampleblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The Valley of Despair}
  \textbf{Year 1:} High confidence. (You don't know what you don't know).
  
  \textbf{Year 3:} The Valley of Despair. Confidence plummets. 
  
  \vspace{0.5cm}
  \textit{Why?} Because you have learned enough to realize how incredibly vast the literature is. A drop in confidence during the middle of a PhD is usually a sign of \textbf{increasing competence}.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Rejection is the Job}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Top journal acceptance rates: 10--20\%.
    \item Grant funding rates: 10--15\%.
    \item \textbf{Rejection is the default state of academia.}
  \end{itemize}
  \vspace{0.3cm}
  If your self-worth is tied to acceptance rates, you will burn out. Rejection is not a measure of your worth as a human; it is simply data indicating a mismatch between a draft and three reviewers.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[standout]
  You are a person who does research.\\
  You are not just a researcher.\\
  De-couple your identity.
\end{frame}
