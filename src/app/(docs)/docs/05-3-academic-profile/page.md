## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:
1. **Establish** a unified academic identity across ORCID, Google Scholar, and institutional pages.
2. **Design** a minimal, effective academic personal website.
3. **Utilize** academic Twitter/X and LinkedIn for strategic networking without falling into time-wasting traps.
4. **Evaluate** the importance of consistent author naming conventions.

---

## Key Concepts

### Concept 1: The Name Problem (ORCID)
- Names change, people share names (e.g., "J. Smith"), and journals format names inconsistently.
- **ORCID (Open Researcher and Contributor ID):** A persistent digital identifier that distinguishes you from every other researcher. It ties all your papers together regardless of name changes or formatting.

### Concept 2: The Google Scholar Profile
- Google Scholar is the most viewed academic profile in the world.
- Setting it up is free, takes 5 minutes, and allows others to easily see all your citations, your h-index, and your most influential work in one place.

### Concept 3: The Academic Website
- You do not need a complex, flashy website. You need a digital business card.
- **The Essentials:** 
  1. A professional photo.
  2. A 2-sentence bio (who you are, what you study).
  3. A list of publications with links to PDFs (Green OA).
  4. Your CV in PDF format.
  5. Contact info.
- Tools: GitHub Pages (free, easy for coders), Notion, or a simple WordPress/Squarespace site.

### Concept 4: Academic Social Media
- **Twitter/X:** The "watercooler" of academia. Great for finding out about new papers, job postings, and conference chatter. The key is to be a curator, not just a broadcaster.
- **LinkedIn:** Increasingly used for industry-adjacent research roles and post-doc networking.
- **ResearchGate/Academia.edu:** Often seen as spammy by senior academics. Better to use Google Scholar + a personal website.

---

## Suggested Hooks & Motivation
- **Pain Point:** You published a great paper, but because the journal formatted your name as "Smith, J. A." instead of "Smith, Jonathan," nobody realizes you wrote it, and it's missing from your tenure review packet.
- **Wow Moment:** Creating a Google Scholar profile and watching all your disparate publications magically group together under your control.
