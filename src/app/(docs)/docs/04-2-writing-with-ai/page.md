## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:
1. **Differentiate** between ethical and unethical uses of Large Language Models (LLMs) in academic writing.
2. **Apply** LLMs as brainstorming, outlining, and editing partners, rather than as ghostwriters.
3. **Evaluate** the risks of AI hallucinations and the necessity of human accountability in research output.
4. **Draft** a transparency statement disclosing the use of AI tools in a manuscript.

---

## Key Concepts

### Concept 1: The "Copilot, Not Autopilot" Principle
- LLMs are statistical pattern matchers, not truth engines. They predict the most likely next word.
- **Unethical use:** Asking an AI to write a literature review or generate data. (This is academic misconduct).
- **Ethical use:** Asking an AI to check grammar, suggest structural improvements, translate from non-native English, or summarize a dense paragraph *that you already wrote*.

### Concept 2: Hallucinations & Accountability
- AI models hallucinate citations. They will invent papers, authors, and DOIs that look perfectly real but do not exist.
- **The Rule of Accountability:** You are 100% responsible for every word and citation in your paper. "The AI made a mistake" is not a defense against plagiarism or falsification charges.

### Concept 3: Prompting for Structure, Not Content
- Bad prompt: "Write an introduction about CRISPR."
- Good prompt: "Here is my draft introduction about CRISPR. Does it follow the IMRaD funnel structure? Point out any sentences that are longer than 25 words."

### Concept 4: Transparency & Disclosure
- Most major publishers (Nature, Science, Elsevier, IEEE) now have explicit AI policies.
- General rule: AI cannot be listed as an author. The use of AI must be explicitly disclosed in the Methods or Acknowledgments section.

---

## Suggested Hooks & Motivation
- **Pain Point:** You want to use ChatGPT to speed up your writing, but you're terrified of getting caught for plagiarism or submitting a hallucinated citation to your advisor.
- **Wow Moment:** Seeing the difference between a lazy "write this for me" prompt (which produces generic, detectable fluff) and a targeted "edit this for me" prompt (which produces sharp, professional academic prose).
