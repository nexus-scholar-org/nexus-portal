## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:
1. **Apply** agile principles (sprints, Kanban boards) to academic research projects.
2. **Deconstruct** a multi-year thesis into manageable 2-week milestones.
3. **Manage** the advisor-advisee relationship using structured update meetings.
4. **Implement** an effective personal knowledge management (PKM) system (e.g., Zettelkasten).

---

## Key Concepts

### Concept 1: The Multi-Year Trap
- A PhD is typically a 4–5 year unstructured project. The lack of intermediate deadlines leads to Parkinson's Law (work expands to fill the time allotted) and burnout.
- **Solution:** Break the thesis down into papers. Break papers down into sections. Break sections down into 2-week "sprints."

### Concept 2: The Academic Kanban Board
- Visualizing work prevents cognitive overload.
- Columns: **Backlog** (Ideas), **To Do** (This Sprint), **Doing** (Right Now), **Waiting** (On Co-authors/Code), **Done**.
- Tools: Trello, Notion, Obsidian, or physical sticky notes.

### Concept 3: Managing Up (The Advisor Relationship)
- Your advisor is not your boss; they are your most important stakeholder and consultant.
- **The Weekly Update:** Never go to a meeting empty-handed. Bring a structured update: What I did, What went wrong, What I'm doing next, What I need from you.

### Concept 4: Personal Knowledge Management (PKM)
- You cannot hold 4 years of literature in your working memory.
- **The Zettelkasten Method:** Taking atomic notes (one idea per note) and linking them together. This ensures that reading today benefits your writing three years from now.

---

## Suggested Hooks & Motivation
- **Pain Point:** You sit down at your desk on Monday morning and think, "I have to write a thesis." It's so overwhelming that you end up answering emails and reading Twitter for three hours instead.
- **Wow Moment:** Seeing a massive thesis broken down into a simple 3-column Kanban board, reducing anxiety instantly.
